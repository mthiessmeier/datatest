# Analytic Framework: Baltic Warning Indicators Following the Ratcliffe Moscow Visit

**Reference time:** 25 August 2026  
**Scope:** Public-source warning analysis only. The framework does not seek to uncover the purpose of an undisclosed or classified U.S. government mission.

## 1. Intelligence questions

**H1:** Was the visit connected to intelligence about a near-term Russian attack, deniable strike, or coercive military provocation against the Baltic states or closely connected NATO infrastructure?

**H2:** If the visit carried a warning, deterrent message, or deconfliction request, did that effort fail?

These are deliberately separated. Evidence supporting H1 does not automatically support H2. H2 should remain provisional while the delegation is still in Russia and should not be inferred merely from secrecy, a missing readout, or Russian public denial.

## 2. Scenario model

### S1 — Deniable UAS/loitering-munition strike

A small number of Shahed/Geran-type, captured Ukrainian, reconstructed Ukrainian-looking, or otherwise ambiguously attributable drones strike Baltic critical infrastructure, a defence-industrial facility, or a transportation/energy node. Russian EW or spoofing may be used to confuse the apparent route and origin. The political objective would be to test NATO attribution and response thresholds without presenting an unmistakable conventional Article 5 case.

This scenario has unusually high current relevance because Lithuanian officials publicly described a Russian false-flag concept involving captured or reconstructed Ukrainian drones against Baltic critical infrastructure, while also stating that they had no indication of an imminent attack.

### S2 — Hybrid-kinetic package

A drone or explosive incident is synchronized with cyber disruption, arson/sabotage, GNSS interference, disinformation, and pressure on border or transportation systems. The combined effect is more diagnostic than any single component.

### S3 — Limited territorial or airspace probe

A short-duration drone, aircraft, maritime, or ground-border incursion tests NATO command-and-control, rules of engagement, and political consultation mechanisms. The action may be framed as navigation error, spillover, or unauthorized activity.

### S4 — Conventional attack preparation

A larger attack would require broader and more visible force, logistics, medical, air-defence, EW, command, and mobilization signatures. Because this has a different force-generation burden, it should not be conflated with S1–S3. Near-term warning for a full conventional assault is currently less supported than warning for a limited, deniable provocation.

## 5. High-value compound signatures

The monitor should prioritize combinations rather than isolated keywords. The following clusters warrant immediate human review:

1. **UAS false-flag cluster:** Russian narrative about Baltic-enabled Ukrainian drone attacks + abrupt GNSS/EW expansion + unidentified drone track + “Ukrainian” debris narrative + incident at infrastructure.
2. **Operational launch cluster:** short-notice closures + western-axis launcher/drone/EW movement + air-defence reinforcement + unusual command/logistics activity.
3. **Failed-warning cluster:** Ratcliffe departure + explicit Russian rejection/hostile readout + immediate NATO force-protection change + operational acceleration within 6–48 hours.
4. **Hybrid package cluster:** cyber/OT disruption + arson/sabotage + drone or airspace incident + synchronized state-media attribution.
5. **Alliance crisis cluster:** specific official threat warning + embassy/civil-defence measures + emergency NAC/Article 4 activity + sustained CAP/AWACS reinforcement.

## 6. Important non-indicators and weak indicators

The following facts may be unusual but should not be treated as evidence that H1 or H2 is true without additional signatures:

- The use of a C-17 or a possible C-40.
- A diplomatic motorcade.
- Kremlin claims of having “no information.”
- White House or CIA silence.
- A request that Ukraine pause strikes while a U.S. delegation is in Russia; this is compatible with ordinary deconfliction.
- Departure without a public readout.
- A planned NATO or Russian exercise by itself.
- A routine Baltic Air Policing intercept.
- A single anonymous social-media post, even if visually compelling.

## 7. Disconfirming evidence

Evidence against H1/H2 must be retained rather than discarded:

- Baltic intelligence/government statements that no direct or imminent threat is detected.
- Demonstrated stand-down, withdrawal, reopening of restricted areas, or return to baseline force posture.
- Independent confirmation that an incident was accidental Ukrainian spillover or ordinary industrial failure.
- Follow-on U.S.–Russian contacts producing observable de-escalation.
- Seventy-two hours after the visit with no new posture change, warning, operational movement, or hostile readout—useful but not dispositive.

## 8. Alert thresholds

**AMBER** is a collection cue. It can result from one substantial but uncorroborated signal.

**ORANGE** requires a score of at least 6 with two independent domains and at least one official/high-reliability source, or a direct official critical warning scoring at least 5.

**RED** requires a score of at least 8 plus either a direct official critical source or a three-domain cluster containing at least two critical indicators.

Social-only evidence cannot exceed AMBER. Scores are not probabilities.

## 9. Initial analytic posture

At reference time, public evidence establishes that CIA Director **John Ratcliffe** traveled to Moscow aboard or in connection with the tracked C-17 movement. It does not establish whom he met, what was discussed, or whether the Baltic region was the subject. Reporting of a C-40 at Riga remains unconfirmed. The most relevant independent warning context is the recent Lithuanian/Polish/Baltic reporting about possible limited kinetic or hybrid attacks on critical infrastructure and the more specific Lithuanian false-flag drone scenario. Those warnings materially justify enhanced collection, but official caveats that no imminent attack had been identified prevent treating them as confirmation of H1.
